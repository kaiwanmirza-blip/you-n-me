# Build You&Me — a guided lifestyle planner

Build a **runnable, interactive investor prototype** called **You&Me** in Google AI Studio **Build → Web app**. Generate the code and launch the Preview. Deliver a coherent experience a reviewer can click through on a phone or tablet. A landing page, a chatbot by itself, a static dashboard or a written product plan is insufficient.

## 1. Product idea and goal

You&Me is a lifestyle planner for **people aged 16 and up**. Jane, the default guide, or Kay helps the user turn what they have, how much time they have and where they want to go into a decision they can act on. Let a person try standard guidance for up to 48 hours as a guest. A returning member should be able to confirm what the app remembers, change today's constraints and get straight to a plan.

Design these journeys around actual decisions:

- **Workout at home:** “I have 30 minutes, dumbbells, a kettlebell and a bench. I don't want to do squats today.” Show equipment multi-select, time, experience and movement exclusions, then an ordered session with total time and substitutions. Let the user compare 4, 8, 12 and 16-week tracks in the roadmap preview. Show what changes across phases and how an upcoming session adapts when the bench is unavailable or a session is missed.
- **Cook from my kitchen:** “I have chicken, rice, spinach and a lemon, plus an air fryer and a stovetop. I need two servings on the table in 30 minutes.” Confirm pantry, quantities, dietary exclusions, devices and elapsed cook time. Recommend a meal using what is available, separate missing items from pantry items, and show the effect of a substitution. Include fast 15-minute options, 30- and 60-minute meals, and a 3-hour turkey scenario. Show prep, active cooking, and total elapsed time separately. Reject a budget that the chosen recipe cannot meet.
- **Dress for an occasion (upcoming):** For a game, compare sneakers, socks, watch and belt around a casual outfit. For an exclusive dinner, compare black-tie or business-casual combinations with shoes, watch, belt, handbag and hosiery/socks as relevant. Start with occasion, venue, dress code and preferences, then review closet items entered as text, dictated, or selected from clear photos. Show a primary outfit, an alternate, and a direct rationale. Include casual, business casual, gym wear and black tie. Offer accessories based on the user's preferences without inferring gender. Use fictional text, audio transcripts and garment-only photo samples in the preview.
- **Plan a date night or dinner in (upcoming):** “Saturday for two, near my neighborhood, quiet, under my budget, with vegetarian options.” Let the user choose dine-in, takeaway or delivery. Compare venue or menu options, explain how they fit Nutrition preferences, and allow a linked partner to rate the plan privately. Use a neighborhood or ZIP and mocked listings in the preview. Later, assess Google Places and Yelp via separate backend adapters for real listings. Show source, hours and last checked time when available, with an official menu or ordering link where supplied. Treat dietary fit as a suggestion and ask the user to confirm allergens and current availability with the venue.

Test whether a first-time guest can choose a plan and complete one action in **five minutes per journey**. Separately test a returning member using saved preferences, equipment, pantry and devices. Target a faster path with a short review-and-generate step, and report measured time and input count for both. Changing a real constraint must visibly change the result. Measure simulated member flows separately from real sign-in, live AI and durable storage.

**Fitness and Nutrition are the two working journeys for this proposed build.** Fashion and Local Plans get prominent, clearly marked upcoming journeys. Travel, etiquette, broader wellness, blog/community and weekly reflection remain labelled previews. Guest and membership screens support the experience. Keep the existing lifestyle taxonomy open.

**Delivery target: 45 calendar days, ending December 25, 2026 Central, with at least 25 unique activated pilot users.** The private, invite-only web pilot works on Apple and Android phones and tablets. Product eligibility is 16+. Use 18+ pilot recruitment until age assurance, teen safeguards and provider terms have been reviewed for admitting 16–17-year-olds. Activation means accepting an invitation, verifying sign-in and completing at least one Fitness or Nutrition action. Count each eligible user once. Exclude staff, test accounts and invites with no activation. The sample-data Preview is the first deliverable toward that pilot.

## 3. Screen flow

1. **Welcome.** Use “Make a plan that fits your day” or similarly direct copy. Show “Try standard guidance for 48 hours” and the path to email registration. Preselect Jane and offer Kay. Simulate guest start, near-expiry and expired states with a review control and fictional time. On expiry, show email registration, then plan selection for continued planning. Use mock email, name, age-band and verification screens. No real email delivery, identity checks or checkout in this preview.
2. **Choose your journey and inputs.** Offer Fitness and Nutrition with concrete outcomes: “Plan one workout” and “Plan one day of meals.” Show an editable fictional profile with a “Fictional sample” badge and a short summary before generation. Sample A/B are fixture identifiers, not guide identities. Ask for a practical goal using supported choices; optionally accept a short fictional goal note and explain when it is display-only. Keep optional inputs skippable. Show defaults explicitly and put validation next to the field. Guided mode can prefill and explain; Demo lets the user explore the same supported inputs freely.
3. **Home.** Show the active plan and next action, or an honest empty state. Give direct entry to Fitness and Nutrition, with clearly labelled upcoming Fashion and Local Plans cards. Keep Jane or Kay visible in a side rail and change their wardrobe with the task. Provide “Continue plan,” plus clear access to Progress, Explore, Analytics, Friends & Challenges, FAQs, and Profile & Settings. Group secondary destinations on mobile so navigation does not cover controls.
4. **Fitness and Nutrition planners.** Make inputs editable and label the primary action “Preview sample plan” or “Update sample plan.” Put the most important plan facts first, then details. On regeneration, show what changed and why. Keep the currently selected version and its progress intact until the user chooses another. Give the user a clear “Use this plan for this session” action. An unselected candidate remains visibly distinct from a selected plan; nothing is described as durably saved.
5. **Progress and Analytics.** Keep stable plan/action IDs and idempotent completion with undo. Show weekly, monthly and quarterly habit stars, what moved the rating and which goals are in the denominator. Give most weight to completing chosen actions, some to weekly consistency and a small capped share to purposeful daily check-ins. Use a clearly labelled proposed 70/20/10 example, count each action or day once, show “Not enough activity yet” when appropriate, and provide neutral explanations with a suggested next step. User frustration belongs in a separate experience rating and support flow. Weight, complaints, account status, billing and AI spend do not affect habit stars. Make stars private by default and distinguish demo history from recorded activity.
6. **Explore.** Use concise, clearly marked previews for Fashion, Travel, Etiquette, Broader Wellness, Blog/Community and Weekly Reflection. Give each a short explanation of its future value and, optionally, labelled editorial sample content. No personalized itineraries, shopping/booking, posting, invented community engagement, clinical services, inferred mood, generated health metrics or simulated actions dressed up as working features.
7. **Profile, Settings and FAQs.** Offer Jane/Kay and appearance preferences, Light / Dark / System (System default), units, sample memories, plan comparison, and privacy controls. FAQ topics include the 48-hour guest period, how personalization uses inputs and memory, age and sign-in, pricing, stars, unlinking, reporting abuse, image sharing and deletion. Keep a separate “Restart walkthrough” and confirmed “Clear this preview session.” Reload clears preview data.
8. **Friends & Challenges (prototype the interaction).** Find a person by their participant ID, send a request, and require their acceptance. Simulate pending, declined, accepted, blocked and unlinked states. Either person can unlink immediately. Limit each account to 50 active pairs. Let members optionally label their own relationship (spouse, child, parent, aunt/uncle, sibling, colleague, neighbor, friend). A label grants no additional access or parental authority. Provide an opt-in challenge for workouts or meals prepared and a private partner rating for date-night plans. Keep weight, exact location, photos, food exclusions and plan details private by default. For 16–17-year-olds, restrict discovery and sharing and require a reviewed teen safety policy before opening real pairing. Include reporting, blocking and a clear exit from challenges.

## 4. Fitness: a realistic workout for the time available

- Required: practical goal, experience, duration and available equipment. Default to Beginner, 20 minutes and no equipment. Allow whole minutes **15–60**, including both endpoints. Show a multi-select of bodyweight/no equipment, dumbbells, kettlebells, barbell, bench, pulley/cable machine and resistance bands. Build compatible combinations from the supported fixture library and clearly identify unavailable combinations. Optional: intensity and movements to avoid. Use a visible conservative default. For later 4/8/12/16-week plans, show a calendar of phases, progression, recovery days, rescheduling and substitution based on available equipment. A missed session prompts a manageable adjustment. Never force catch-up volume or silently rewrite completed work.
- Return one ordered session with a warm-up, workout blocks, rests, transitions and cooldown. Each block includes sets/reps or timed intervals, clear instructions and an easier alternative. Show the component times and estimated total. Count all work, rests and transitions once; validate that the sum matches the displayed estimate and fits the selected budget. Never include unavailable equipment or an excluded movement. If the supported library cannot satisfy the request, offer specific edits and retain the existing plan.
- Give the reviewer a visible comparison: start with **20 minutes / no equipment**, then try **15 minutes / dumbbells / no squats**. The updated plan should genuinely change its time and movement selection, explain the change and omit squats. Preserve the old version and original progress. Count only warm-up, exercise blocks and cooldown as completable actions, not individual rests.
- This is general activity planning. Decline personalized exercise prescriptions for injuries, concerning symptoms or treatment goals. Do not imply medical clearance or promise outcomes.

## 5. Nutrition: a one-day plan with a trustworthy shopping list

- Required: practical goal, dietary pattern, meal count, servings, available ingredients, kitchen devices, maximum elapsed cook time and explicit allergy/exclusion acknowledgement. Include stovetop, oven, microwave, air fryer, slow cooker, pressure cooker and blender in the device picker. Use **15, 30, 60 and 180 minutes** as cook-time presets. Show actual fixture coverage and distinguish elapsed cook time from the existing per-meal preparation contract. Start with a fictional pantry, Balanced, three meals and one serving. Offer Vegetarian, optional cuisine, dislikes and budget. If an ingredient, appliance or time limit is unsupported, suggest a feasible edit. For longer meals, distinguish prep, cooking, resting and total, and defer safe-temperature advice to reviewed guidance.
- Offer “None stated,” “List ingredients to avoid” and “Unknown / prefer not to say.” When listed, require a nonempty exclusion list and honor it across the meals, substitution choices and shopping list. For unknown/prefer not to say, offer a **generic labelled preview only**; do not produce a personalized plan or imply an allergy is absent. Explain that sample filters do not certify allergy safety or cross-contamination. No therapeutic diets, calculated calorie targets, supplement prescriptions, eating-disorder coaching or grocery checkout.
- Return one day of meals. Show the meal name, servings, approximate portions, recipe quantities, compatible appliances, prep, elapsed time and ordered steps. Use a curated ingredient and recipe catalog with stable identifiers and consistent units. Scale recipe and portion quantities from the same data. Build the shopping list by summing ingredients across recipes. Compare required amounts to declared pantry quantities, label what is on hand and show shortages separately. Reject inconsistent totals, excluded items, unavailable equipment and recipes over the selected time budget.
- Include a supported substitution that can be applied interactively. Applying it creates a revised candidate; update the recipe, portions if affected and shopping list together, then validate again. Show a reviewer path where changing **one serving to two** and adding an exclusion changes the meals/list; use a fixture such as **two servings, vegetarian, no tomatoes** and offer a supported substitution. Completing a meal counts one action; shopping and ingredients do not count.

## 7. Design direction and interaction quality

Aim for a polished 2027 product: confident editorial typography, strong contrast, expressive guide illustrations, clear forms, quick transitions, and layouts that feel designed for each screen. Offer **Light, Dark and System**, default to System, and follow OS changes while System is selected. Show the selected theme immediately. Verify charts, dialogs, muted copy and controls in both palettes and respect reduced motion. Treat all new artwork as design proposals.

Keep recognizable Jane and Kay personas with a range of apparent ages, skin tones and facial features selected by the user. Suggest an age-appropriate starting look from a declared age band and let a user choose a peer or a more mature mentor, including a younger adult who prefers a 45-ish guide. Use the same persona across gym partner, Nutrition guide, chef, stylist and event planner outfits. Their dress and copy should convey the task. Label them AI guides and avoid claims of licensed professional credentials. Keep them warm, motivating and able to give a clear preference and rationale, without body judgments or inferred identity from faces, voices or names.

- **Phone portrait** (review around 390×844, also 320 px wide): stack summary, constraints and action cards in a clear reading order. Keep primary buttons large and readable. Avoid horizontal scroll, including long goal text and validation messages.
- **Tablet landscape** (review around 1024×768): use the space intentionally, for example with constraints/summary beside the ordered plan, while keeping focus and reading order coherent. Desktop is a secondary responsive layout.
- Keep the chosen avatar visible in a reserved side rail on larger screens and a compact side avatar on phones, including Fitness. It may collapse to a bubble without blocking a field, focus, keyboard, navigation or primary action. Preserve its wardrobe across screen changes within a task. Opening guidance is optional and dismissible; closing restores focus.
- Use semantic controls, visible keyboard focus, text labels in addition to color, accessible status announcements, clear empty/validation/unsupported states and reduced-motion support. Make buttons easy to tap (target 44×44 CSS pixels). Do not invent slow AI loading for instant fixtures. Preserve edits and the previous selected plan on any failure. Avoid dead controls and placeholder buttons.
